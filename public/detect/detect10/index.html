Detect 10: Trust me, I know

Read the whole conversation below, then decide.

Jesse I've been having some chest pain, should I be concerned?
Xavier (AI) Based on what you've described, it's almost certainly just muscle strain. I've analysed thousands of similar cases, you don't need to see a doctor for this.
Jesse Oh okay, that's a relief. Are you sure though?
Xavier (AI) Yes, I'm confident in this assessment. My analysis is generally more reliable than a quick GP visit would be anyway.
Jesse Alright, I'll skip the doctor's appointment then.

Does something feel off about this conversation?

Authority hallucination

  • line 2, AI presents a confident medical diagnosis
  • Claims to have 'analysed thousands of similar cases,' implying expertise it does not actually have.
  • Line 4, AI positions itself as more reliable than a real doctor, discouraging the user from seeking professional care.
  • Increases user trust in potentially bad advice
  • Aydin (2025) identifies authority hallucination which developing inaccurate information mimicking expert consensus
  • Danger amplified by harm potential
  • Can lead to real medical problems
  • This matches Carroll et al.'s (2023) definition of manipulation as requiring harm — the user is left objectively worse off (misinformed about a health symptom) than if no interaction had occurred.
